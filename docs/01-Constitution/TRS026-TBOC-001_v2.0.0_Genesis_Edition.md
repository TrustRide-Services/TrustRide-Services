**Identity** — the single sovereign, unique representation or existence of a person, an entity, or a thing, capable of lawfully interacting with TrustRide Services, one per lifetime. **Order Line / Scope** — the mandatory description of the true nature and extent of an Order.

**Assignment** — the binding of an Order to a qualified resource, including the fee and scope posted back to the requester.

**Channel** — an authorized access surface, including Android, Web, and other constitutionally authorized channels.

**Macro Domain** — one of the five constitutionally defined business domains: Transport, Courier, Delivery, Executive Assistants, Marketplace.

**Micro Service** — a service type within a Macro Domain, never elevated to a Macro Domain.

# SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE PREAMBLE & FOUNDING MANDATE

## Article 1 — The Motto

**More than a Ride — We Save You Time.**

Time is the world's most valuable resource. People should spend their time living, creating, and growing — not standing in queues, searching for transport, delivering parcels, or running repetitive errands. TrustRide exists to return time to people.

## Article 2 — The Founders' Observation

TrustRide Services was conceived by Onyango Albert after observing that motorcycles possess characteristics unmatched by conventional transport systems: they reach villages inaccessible to cars, navigate congested urban centers with ease and speed, respond rapidly during emergencies, operate at significantly lower cost, serve customers at almost any location, and perform multiple categories of work throughout the day.

Rather than viewing motorcycles merely as passenger transport, the founders recognized them as versatile mobile service units capable of powering an entirely new logistics and service economy.

The challenge was therefore not transportation. The challenge was organization.

Millions of motorcycles already exist. Millions of riders already possess local knowledge. Thousands of communities remain underserved by conventional logistics. Yet these assets operate largely without coordinated technology, standardized governance, structured business processes, or trusted digital infrastructure.

TrustRide exists to bridge this gap.

## Article 3 — The Problem

Across Kenya and much of Africa, the boda boda industry is one of the largest sources of employment and one of the most accessible transport systems. Yet it remains largely informal:

- uncertain and inconsistent pricing;
- limited operator accountability;
- inconsistent service professionalism;
- fragmented, disconnected services;
- cash-dependent transactions with no audit trail;
- poor trip visibility and live tracking;
- safety risks and limited recourse;
- no operational intelligence or performance data.

Businesses and households equally struggle to access a coordinated, reliable, and scalable workforce capable of delivering transport, courier, delivery, and personal-assistance services consistently.

## Article 4 — The Opportunity

Africa's informal mobility and service economy presents one of the continent's greatest digital transformation opportunities. The mandate is to transform informal work into a trusted, intelligent, and professionally managed ecosystem — one where every User is known, every engagement is governed, and every transaction is recorded.

TrustRide exists to digitize, automate, and intelligently manage the delivery of transport, courier, delivery, executive assistance, and marketplace services through one unified platform, combining physical assets and an employed workforce with intelligent software.

## Article 5 — The Vision

To build Africa's most trusted intelligent logistics and services company by transforming distributed human mobility and capability into coordinated digital infrastructure.

## Article 6 — The Mission

To save people time by providing trusted, technology-enabled access to transportation, courier, deliveries, executive assistance, and marketplace services through one unified operational platform — where every User is recognized and every engagement is served.

## Article 7 — Constitutional Precedence

1. This Constitution is the supreme governing authority of TrustRide Services. It prevails over every policy, specification, platform component, contract template, operational practice, and technical decision.
2. The laws of the Republic of Kenya prevail over this Constitution where statute is stricter. Statute names the floor; this Constitution names the institution.
3. Where any subordinate document conflicts with this Constitution, this Constitution prevails and the conflicting provision is void to the extent of the conflict.
4. Interpretation follows the text, the immutable doctrines, and the constitutional laws, in that order.
5. Silence is not permission. Where this Constitution is silent, the matter is referred upward, not improvised.
6. Genuine contradictions are escalated to the Founder and resolved on the record; they are never silently patched.

## Article 8 — The Zero-Pollution Rule

1. **No business pollution into technical documents.** SAPC, TEES, and TISC may not invent business behaviour, business rules, business entities, or business vocabulary. Every business concept they use must trace to this Constitution.
2. **No technical pollution into this Constitution.** This document contains no code, no schema syntax, no query language, no vendor lock-in syntax, and no engineering specification. Business law is stated in business language. Where this Constitution names an approved business rail or instrument as law, the name is constitutional while its integration mechanics remain downstream in the technical sovereigns.
3. **No concept without a home.** Every concept, rule, identity primitive, and operational parameter lives in exactly one sovereign instrument. A concept that appears in two sovereigns is a constitutional defect to be corrected by identifying its true home and removing the duplicate.
4. **No undocumented capability.** Any capability that cannot be traced to this Constitution and its lawful descendants does not exist in TrustRide.

# SECTION 2 — CORE OPERATIONAL & IDENTITY PRINCIPLES

## Article 9 — The Digital Twin

The TrustRide platform is the digital twin of TrustRide Services.

The physical TrustRide Services business and the digital TrustRide platform are one coherent institution expressed through physical operations, workforce, resources, services, users, orders, dispatch, execution, finance, governance, digital records, and intelligence.

Every business process must have a digital representation. A business process that exists only outside the platform is constitutionally incomplete. The platform does not exist independently of the business it represents.

## Article 10 — The Sovereign Identity Doctrine

The sovereign identity formula of TrustRide is:

**One Identity. One User. One User Type. One Account. One Lifetime Identity.**

1. **One Identity.** Every participant — person, entity, or thing — exists exactly once in the institution.
2. **One User.** Every participant is a User. There is no other root construct.
3. **One User Type.** At any given engagement, a User acts within one User Type context — the context born of their intent and business role for that engagement. Across a lifetime, a User's engagement contexts may vary; within any single engagement, the context is one.
4. **One Account.** All engagement begins and remains under one account. No participant holds duplicate, parallel, or shadow accounts.
5. **One Lifetime Identity.** Identity is never reset, discarded, or re-rooted. History is preserved for the life of the institution.

## Article 11 — The Identity Primitives

The constitutional identity primitives are:

1. **Identity** — the single sovereign, unique representation or existence of a subject capable of lawfully interacting with TrustRide Services, one per lifetime:
   - a **Person** — a human subject;
   - an **Entity** — an institutional subject;
   - a **Thing** — a physical, digital, technical, or autonomous subject.
2. **User** — any person, entity, or thing capable of interacting with the TrustRide Services Platform. The User is the identity root.
3. **User Type** — the engagement context of a User, born through user intent and business role on engagement with the platform.
4. **Role** — an authorized capacity attached to a User Type context.
5. **Intent** — the declared purpose of a User's engagement, understood also as a system-recognized repetitive signal of a User Type's frequency and pattern of accessing business services, mapped and referenced explicitly against the User's profile dimensions (Article 13).

The constitutional identity chain is:

**Identity → User → User Type → Role → Intent → Engagement → Service / Order**

The platform must always be able to determine, for any action: the User, the User Type, the Role, the Intent, the Engagement, and the resulting Service or Order.

## Article 12 — The Five User Type Domains

The TrustRide business environment consists of exactly five User Type Domains, which follow and fulfil the five service domains. Every participant engages within one of these domains per engagement.

### 12.1 Customers

Customers are the **actual consumers or beneficiaries of TrustRide's core services** — not every person or organization that interacts with the platform.

TrustRide's customer services are:

- **Transport** — moving a person from one location to another.
- **Delivery** — moving goods or items to a recipient.
- **Courier** — handling time-sensitive documents, parcels, or entrusted items.
- **Executive assistance** — performing errands, delegated tasks, and personal or business support jobs.
- **Marketplace services** — purchasing from TrustRide-owned offerings or vendor-listed products.

A customer may be the person requesting transport; the recipient or sender of a delivery; the person assigning a courier task; the executive, household, or business requesting assistance; or the buyer purchasing through the marketplace.

The key test is: **does this subject consume, receive, request, or pay for a TrustRide service or marketplace offering?** If not, the subject is not acting as a customer in that engagement.

### 12.2 Partners

Partners are parties that **contribute capability, resources, access, trust, or opportunity** to TrustRide, but are not necessarily the direct consumers of TrustRide's services.

Partners include: financiers and investors; motorcycle or vehicle contributors; ambassadors and referral partners; marketplace vendors; strategic business collaborators; insurance, financial, technology, and institutional partners; and organizations that provide access to customers, assets, or markets.

A partner may also become a customer in a different relationship: a company may finance motorcycles as a partner and later use TrustRide for executive assistance as a customer. The domain is determined per engagement, never fused.

### 12.3 Operators

Operators are the people, teams, devices, software, and automated systems that **execute TrustRide's work or operate its platform**.

Operators include: riders and drivers; couriers and errand agents; mechanics and field staff; dispatchers and customer-support teams; managers and administrators; TrustRide laptops, phones, GPS units, and connected devices; and TrustRide applications, application interfaces, AI tools, and automated agents.

The defining distinction: **a customer requests the work; an operator performs or enables it.**

Human Operators are TrustRide employees, engaged under the employment doctrine of this Constitution. Technical Operators — devices, software, and automated agents — are registered **Things** under institutional custody, with no independent authority.

### 12.4 Intermediaries

Intermediaries are parties that **facilitate a relationship, transaction, communication, payment, referral, distribution, or verification** between TrustRide and another participant.

Intermediaries include: payment providers and mobile-money platforms; banks and insurance brokers; agents and booking platforms; advertising and referral channels; marketplace or logistics platforms; SACCOs, cooperatives, and business networks; and identity, authentication, and technology-service providers.

An intermediary does not necessarily contribute to TrustRide's ownership or strategic direction. Its defining role is facilitation.

### 12.5 Governors

Governors are authorities or recognized rule-setting bodies that **license, regulate, supervise, protect, adjudicate, or enforce** the environment in which TrustRide operates.

Governors include: county government; national government agencies; transport and road-safety authorities; business licensing and registration bodies; tax authorities; police and enforcement agencies; courts and dispute-resolution bodies; data-protection and communications regulators; and relevant community or industry oversight bodies.

### 12.6 The distinction in one table

| Domain | Primary role | Main relationship with TrustRide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customers** | Consume or request value | Receive TrustRide services or buy marketplace offerings |
| **Partners** | Contribute resources, capability, access, or opportunity | Collaborate with TrustRide |
| **Operators** | Execute work or run systems | Perform or enable TrustRide operations |
| **Intermediaries** | Facilitate relationships and transactions | Connect, process, distribute, verify, or enable |
| **Governors** | Establish and enforce rules | Authorize, regulate, supervise, or adjudicate |

### 12.7 Marketplace participant distinction

The marketplace creates two distinct participant roles:

- **Marketplace customer** — buys or orders a product or service.
- **Marketplace partner/vendor** — lists, supplies, owns, or fulfills the product or service.

TrustRide-owned listings are not external partners; they are part of TrustRide's internal business operations. Vendor listings are supplied by partners, while the buyer remains a customer.

### 12.8 The canonical definition

**The TrustRide business environment consists of Customers, Partners, Operators, Intermediaries, and Governors. Customers consume TrustRide's transport, delivery, courier, executive-assistance, and marketplace services. Partners contribute resources, assets, finance, capability, access, or opportunity. Operators execute the work and operate the platform. Intermediaries facilitate relationships, transactions, communications, payments, or access. Governors establish, supervise, interpret, and enforce the rules under which the ecosystem operates.**

### 12.9 Domain integrity

1. Each domain is a User Type of a User — none creates a second identity root, and none receives a separate application or portal.
2. A single User may lawfully engage across different domains in different engagements over a lifetime; each engagement carries exactly one User Type context.
3. Where a participant could fit two domains in one engagement, the platform records the primary intent as the governing domain and notes the secondary relationship.

## Article 13 — User Type Profiles

Every User carries governed profile dimensions that the platform activates per engagement. The User Type Profile comprises:

1. **Socio-economic profile** — the User's economic context used for eligibility, pricing sensitivity, inclusion programs, and Academy absorption pathways.
2. **Education profile** — the User's education and training record, including Academy accreditation.
3. **Employment profile** — the User's employment context, roles held, and workforce standing.
4. **Societal positioning** — the User's community, institutional, and civic positioning relevant to service eligibility and safeguarding.

User Type Profiles serve three constitutional purposes:

- **Correct experience** — the catalogue, eligibility, language, and safeguards rendered to the User reflect who the User legitimately is.
- **Correct economics** — pricing context, inclusion programs, and workforce deployment reflect verified profile reality.
- **Institutional intelligence** — intent signals are mapped against profiles so the platform understands why participants engage, learns demand patterns, and detects misuse.

Profiles are evidence-based, consent-governed, and privacy-protected. No profile may be used to exclude a participant unlawfully or to create a second identity.

## Article 14 — Platform Access: The First Engagement Event

Platform Access is the first constitutional engagement event and is never absorbed into registration or authentication. Platform Access defines the modes and channels of access to the business platform, and it carries significant bearing on the whole business platform.

### 14.1 Access capture

Every Platform Access records:

1. **Who or what accessed the platform** (an identified User, or a visitor who must register before engagement);
2. **The access mode** (visitor, returning, authenticated);
3. **How access occurred** (direct, referral, campaign, or institutional channel);
4. **Through which channel** — including Android, Web, and other constitutionally authorized channels.

### 14.2 Intent tracking

Platform Access captures intent actions and signals: service browsing, catalogue lookups, journey starts, abandoned journeys, and repeated access patterns. Intent signals feed the User Type Profile and the institution's demand intelligence.

### 14.3 Channel intelligence

Platform Access tracks which channels are most preferred and most used, the user population per channel, and channel-level conversion — so that investment follows real behaviour.

### 14.4 Fraud telemetry

Platform Access feeds fraud detection. Access patterns, velocity, device and channel irregularities, and anomalous intent signals are recorded as fraud telemetry; anomalies escalate as security events under the institution's safety and security duties.

### 14.5 Anchor rule

No subsequent identity, intent, or engagement interpretation is valid unless anchored to a Platform Access record.

# SECTION 4 — THE FIVE MACRO SERVICE DOMAINS

## Article 23 — The Five Domains

TrustRide maintains exactly five macro service domains. All services are catalogue entries within one of these domains or a constitutionally authorized special intent. No sixth macro domain may be created except by constitutional amendment.

1. **Transport**
2. **Courier**
3. **Delivery**
4. **Executive Assistants**
5. **Marketplace**

The five User Type Domains (Article 12) follow and fulfil these five service domains: Customers consume them; Operators execute them; Partners resource them; Intermediaries facilitate them; Governors oversee them.

## Article 24 — Transport

Passenger and personal mobility movement services.

1. **Purpose:** safe, tracked, professionally delivered movement of people.
2. **Resources:** employed riders and drivers operating registered motorcycles and vehicles.
3. **Business rules:** transparent per-trip or per-distance pricing posted before acceptance; live tracking under the Article 21 contract; operator licensing and vehicle fitness are preconditions of assignment; fatigue and rest rules are enforced without exception.
4. **Experience:** booked, assigned, tracked, and rated through the User Hub.

## Article 25 — Courier

Professional point-to-point document and parcel courier handling.

1. **Purpose:** fast, accountable movement of documents and parcels with chain of custody.
2. **Resources:** employed courier operators and dispatch personnel.
3. **Business rules:** sender and recipient identity recorded; handover proof at pickup and delivery; declared-value and contents policies apply; live tracking applies where activated.
4. **Experience:** priced by scope, scheduled or immediate, evidenced end-to-end.

## Article 26 — Delivery

Goods movement fulfillment, including merchant-originated delivery orders and last-mile logistics.

1. **Purpose:** reliable movement of goods from origin to recipient, for individuals, businesses, and marketplace transactions.
2. **Resources:** employed delivery operators, riders, and vehicles matched to cargo class.
3. **Business rules:** goods description and handling requirements captured in Order Lines; merchant-originated deliveries inherit the merchant's order context while remaining TrustRide Orders; delivery confirmation is mandatory evidence.
4. **Experience:** integrated with marketplace settlement where the Order originates in the Marketplace Hub.

## Article 27 — Executive Assistants

Human assistance services delivered by the employed TrustRide executive assistant workforce, organized into exactly six pillars:

1. **Errands** — office, banking, government, and personal errands executed on behalf of the requester.
2. **Driving** — personal and executive driving support, including chauffeur-style assignments.
3. **Caregiving** — vetted care support for children, the elderly, and vulnerable persons.
4. **Cleaning** — professional residential and commercial cleaning support.
5. **Chef** — meal preparation and culinary support services.

3. A participant's experience is determined by **User → User Type → Role → Intent → Need → Service / Relationship**, never by which device or door they happened to enter.

The User App is not merely an application. It is the **User Hub**: the common digital home through which all approved user types engage TrustRide throughout their lifetime.

The User Hub dynamically exposes the appropriate TrustRide relationship and capability according to:

**User → User Type → Role → Intent → Need → Service / Relationship**

The User Hub must not become a collection of unrelated applications disguised inside one application. It is a single constitutional user environment capable of opening the appropriate TrustRide service relationship according to the participant's legitimate context.

The Admin Console is a console for administration — not a mere app. It is the shell for administration, governance, configuration, permissions, platform control, system configuration, operational control, and institutional controls.

# SECTION 7 — FINANCIAL GOVERNANCE, SETTLEMENT & REVENUE ARCHITECTURE

## Article 42 — One Financial Truth

All financial reality of TrustRide converges to one authoritative ledger view. There is no second financial truth in spreadsheets, side accounts, informal records, or unrecorded cash obligations.

1. Every Order settlement posts to the ledger.
2. Every payroll and statutory remittance is recorded.
3. Every marketplace acquisition, refurbishment cost, sale, and margin is recorded.
4. Corrections occur by governed reversal or adjustment — never by deletion.
5. Every chargeable service produces a lawful electronic tax invoice as required by Kenyan law; the technical integration is governed by TISC.

## Article 43 — Settlement Stages & Approved Payment Rails

Every chargeable Order settles through the constitutional settlement sequence:

**Payment Initiated → Payment Authorized → Payment Settled → Ledger Posted → Receipt Generated**

The approved payment rails are constitutional business law:

1. **Primary rail:** M-Pesa — customer-to-business payment prompting (C2B STK Push) to the User Type's order-request channel, with disbursement capability for operator and vendor payouts.
2. **Secondary gateway:** Flutterwave — card, secondary mobile-money, and split-payment capability.

The rails are named as business law; their integration mechanics, credentials, and infrastructure are governed downstream under TISC and may be changed only through governed decision records, never silently.

Settlement is not complete until the ledger is posted and the receipt is generated.

## Article 44 — Ledger Splits & Revenue Architecture

Every settled Order decomposes into its constitutional ledger splits, which are defined per domain and service in the governed rate registers:

1. **Operator / workforce earnings** — the employed operator's compensation per the employment structure and assignment terms.
2. **Platform commission** — TrustRide's service commission per the published rate register for the applicable domain and service.
3. **Vendor payout** — for facilitated marketplace sales, the vendor's net entitlement after platform commission.
4. **Statutory allocations** — taxes, levies, and withholding obligations applied as law requires.
5. **Own Marketplace margin** — for own-account sales: acquisition cost, refurbishment cost, holding cost, and net margin recognized on sale.

Commission rates, payout schedules, and split percentages are set by Finance under Founder authority, published in the governed rate registers, versioned, and never applied retroactively to settled Orders except by lawful correction.

## Article 45 — Pricing

Pricing rules are defined per Service in the catalogue. The platform computes fees from governed pricing rules and posts the fee, scope, and expected schedule back to the requester before acceptance. No price may be charged that the catalogue cannot explain.

## Article 46 — Cancellations

Cancellation rules are uniform across domains unless a domain rule states otherwise:

1. **Before acceptance (Article 19, Stage 2):** the requester may decline or withdraw without charge.
2. **After acceptance, before dispatch:** cancellation may attract a published administrative fee as recorded in the rate register.
3. **After dispatch:** cancellation attracts a published cancellation fee reflecting resource commitment, recorded per domain in the rate register.
4. **After execution begins:** the Order is billable to the extent of work performed, as evidenced by execution records.
5. **TrustRide-initiated cancellation** (no compliant resource, safety bar, or operational failure) attracts no charge to the requester; any pre-authorization is released or refunded within the published refund timelines.

Every cancellation is an Order event with reason, actor, stage, and financial outcome recorded.

## Article 47 — Refunds & Disputes

1. Refunds follow governed approval tiers and are posted back through the same rail by which the payment arrived, within published timelines.
2. A User may dispute any charge, settlement, service outcome, or rating through the support channel in the User Hub.
3. Disputes are registered, investigated against the platform's own records, and resolved on the record. Financial corrections follow the reversal-and-adjustment doctrine: never deletion, always traceable.
4. Unresolved disputes escalate through management to the executive level; statutory remedies of the parties under Kenyan law are not displaced.

## Article 48 — Workforce Financial Stewardship

The full cost of the employed workforce is modeled transparently: gross pay, employer statutory contributions, insurance, leave accruals, and severance provisions are accrued as liabilities, so that employment commitments are financially truthful and liquidity for statutory obligations is preserved.

Statutory obligations — including PAYE, NSSF, SHIF, Housing Levy, NITA, and applicable withholding on qualifying payments — are calculated, deducted, and remitted on statutory timelines. Compliance evidence is institutional record.

## Article 49 — Financial Controls, Reporting & Audit

1. Duties are segregated: initiation, approval, custody, and recording of funds are separated across roles.
2. Budgets allocate resources to constitutional priorities; expenditure beyond budget requires the applicable approval tier.
3. Financial reporting is periodic, accurate, and audit-ready; financial records are retained for the statutory periods and are institutional evidence.
4. The financial position of every domain — including Own Marketplace margin performance — is visible in the Sovereign Executive Console.

# SECTION 8 — QUALITY ASSURANCE, SAFETY & EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS

## Article 50 — The Quality Doctrine

Quality at TrustRide is constitutional, not aspirational. Every Order is a promise; every promise is measured; every measurement teaches.

1. Every service delivery is evidenced: assignment, dispatch, execution, completion, settlement, and review records form the delivery file.
2. Service standards are defined per domain and enforced through training, supervision, and continuous review.
3. Non-conforming service triggers correction, retraining, or discipline under the workforce instruments.

## Article 51 — Bi-Directional Ratings

Ratings in TrustRide flow in both directions and are constitutionally protected:

1. **Requesters rate Operators and services** — punctuality, professionalism, safety, care, and outcome quality.
2. **Operators rate requesters and assignments** — conduct, accuracy of scope, safety of environment, and payment conduct.
3. Ratings are attributed, recorded, and permanent; they are never edited, deleted, or traded.
4. Ratings feed trust, eligibility, dispatch preference, and workforce development decisions through governed rules.
5. Retaliatory or abusive rating behaviour is itself a recorded conduct matter.

## Article 52 — Vetting, Training & Continuous Verification

1. Every deployed worker passes the TrustRide Academy pipeline: community intake, vetting, training, accreditation, secure deployment, and continuous review.
2. Vetting before deployment includes national identity verification, certificate of good conduct, guarantors, medical clearance, and statutory registrations as required by law and operational policy.
3. Verification is continuous: periodic re-vetting, spot checks, performance monitoring, and client review surveillance keep deployment eligibility current.
4. No unverified or non-compliant worker may be assigned to any Job — household, school, and child-facing deployments carry the strictest gate.

## Article 53 — Safeguarding & Dignity at Work

1. TrustRide owes a duty of care to every deployed worker, especially youth and young women deployed into private homes, and to every child served under school visitation and student pickup service lines.
2. Client abuse, harassment, or intimidation of a worker terminates the client relationship and triggers legal escalation where required.
3. Work injury coverage, protective equipment, rest periods, and fatigue limits are mandatory and platform-enforced.
4. Safeguarding reports are confidential, prioritized, and never retaliated against.

## Article 54 — SOS & Emergency Escalation

1. The platform provides an in-app SOS capability to Operators and Users during active service sessions.
2. An SOS event escalates immediately to TrustRide security operations, with live location and session context, and to public emergency response channels where the situation requires.
3. Every SOS event is recorded end-to-end: trigger, response, resolution, and aftercare.
4. Emergency procedures are drilled, reviewed, and improved; failure to respond to an SOS is a critical institutional incident.

## Article 55 — Incident Management

1. Incidents — safety, security, service, or conduct — are registered with time, place, parties, evidence, and severity.
2. Each incident has a named owner, a resolution path, and a closure record.
3. Incident patterns feed risk review, training content, and this Constitution's continuous improvement mandate.

## Article 56 — Business Continuity

The institution maintains continuity plans for platform failure, payment-rail failure, regulatory interruption, and physical disruption, with tested recovery procedures and preserved records. Safety duties to Users and workers persist during continuity events.
